The plan is the approved office-hours design for a firedrill board for an enterprise IT lead: a custom app on OpenShift with pull-based email intake, human-confirmed field extraction and manager updates. This summary records only what the CEO review decided on top of it. The plan's own "CEO Review" section holds the decision ledger and each question as it was asked.

## Vision

### 10x Check

The board runs the loop. In the approved plan the boss still does the asking and managers still have to adopt a new screen. In the 10x version the board asks when something turns stale, managers answer by replying to the email, what they need from him surfaces at the top of the board, and on Monday the whole picture arrives in his inbox. That is roughly twice the build for a tool he never has to remember to use.

### Platonic Ideal

He forwards an email and forgets about it. A draft appears with every field backed by a quote, he picks owners in two clicks, and from then on status arrives without anyone asking. Each manager answers in one sentence from wherever they already are. When a manager is stuck on him, he sees it within the hour. Nothing on the board is ever older than he has agreed to tolerate, and when it is, the board has already asked. After a quarter he can show his own boss where the firedrills come from.

## Accepted Scope (added to this plan)

Milestone 1:

- A "Needs you" list at the top of the board showing every open need a manager has raised, with its age and a Handled action. Everyone signed in sees the list. The manager who raised a need sees who handled it and when, and can withdraw it.
- Four status links in the ask (on track, at risk, blocked, done), each opening the update screen with that status already chosen. If the links do not survive the boss's Outlook, the ask falls back to one link.
- Ticket refs shown as links to the ticket, built from one ServiceNow address setting. The implementation owner must prove the link format for each ticket prefix.
- An overdue badge with days past due, an overdue count, and undated firedrills sorted after dated ones.
- "Waiting until" a date with a required reason, set by the owner, a Lead or Triage. A waiting assignment is not stale, is not asked automatically, and is left out of the freshness figures.
- A "Seen by" mark with name and time on each update a Lead has viewed, visible to the update's author and to the owner it was posted for.

Milestone 2:

- Reply-to-update. App-sent asks come from the app's shared mailbox, set Reply-To to that mailbox and carry a reference the app can match. A reply from the assignment's owner becomes an update labeled "via email". The status changes only when the first non-empty line is exactly one of the four status words. The manager gets a receipt quoting what was posted. Automatic mail such as out-of-office replies is never posted. If no reference can be matched on this mail system, the feature does not ship.
- Automatic asks. One ask per stale period, sent at 7:00 on the working day the assignment turns stale, none while waiting, none when an unanswered ask exists, with an off switch per firedrill and a global switch that starts off.
- Monday digest. One email to the primary Lead at 7:00 each Monday listing firedrills published in the last seven days, stale assignments, what is due this week or overdue, and open needs. A week with all four lists empty sends a short all-clear.
- Sending and failures. Every app-sent email has a recorded state, is retried when it fails, and shows in a banner when it cannot be sent.

Consequences recorded in the plan:

- Milestone 4 now holds history only: a per-firedrill timeline and a list of closed firedrills.
- Milestone 2 additionally waits on read and write access to the mailbox and on permission for the app to send mail as that shared mailbox.
- Effort was re-estimated: M0 about half a day, M1 about 3 weeks for a human team or 2 days with Claude Code, M2 about 3 to 4 weeks or 2 days, not counting the proofs owed on reply matching, reply stripping and the sender address.
- Milestone 2 starts when its approvals land. The stop rule pauses everything after M2 and never pauses reply-to-update.
- Three open questions were added: whether the boss agrees to automatic asks and to managers seeing "Needs you" ages and "Seen by" marks (15), whether replies can be matched to an assignment on this mail system (16), and whether managers will answer by replying (17). The Assignment now asks the boss and one manager these directly.
